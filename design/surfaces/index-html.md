# Surface brief: index.html (the midterm desk)

Scope: one published artifact page. Visitor mode: Read, with an Operate section (the night tracker and the sortable tables).
Audience and job: the owner, one analyst-reader, overnight IST on 3–4 Nov 2026. Job: understand the call and the evidence, check any state in two scrolls, then follow results in order.
Content: three research reports (Senate, House, national) plus a live Polymarket/Kalshi snapshot. Constraints: static snapshot, every number sourced and dated, opinion labelled, both parties' cases present, light and dark themes, phone width.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The optical-scan ballot is the one artefact every American voter handles on this day, and its grammar (contest-header bands, heavy rules, the fill-in oval) can carry a results desk: a filled oval is a call, a half-filled oval is a lean, an empty oval is open. It refuses the category default, the red-blue probability dashboard with cards and a choropleth map.

OWN-WORLD: Ballot stock (a warm paper off-white, tinted toward the ink, not cream) with near-black ink; one civic accent, the ballot-header slate blue used by county election offices, plus the two party hues held to small areas. Contest headers are reversed black bands with the title in bold caps. Heavy 2–3px rules separate contests; thin rules separate candidates. The left edge carries a scanner timing-mark rail that doubles as section navigation. Type: a civic humanist sans for everything (Public Sans, the US government face), with Source Serif 4 for the long-form analysis column. Dark mode is the tabulator's screen: dark slate ground, pale ink, the same ovals.

STORY: The reader understands the bottom line in one screen (the control contests as two filled/half ovals), believes it because three independent reads sit side by side with their sources, then checks their own states and follows the night in IST order.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Masthead band (desk name, date stamp, snapshot time). Below it the two control contests set as ballot contests: "Control of the Senate" and "Control of the House", each with the two parties as candidate rows, an oval filled to the author's call, and three small reads to the right (ratings consensus, markets, author). Under them the one-paragraph author's call and the contents rail. No hero image, no giant number tile.
