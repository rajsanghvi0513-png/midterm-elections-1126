House, state by state

435 seats · 218 for a majority · the first midterm fought on mid-decade maps in both directions

Republicans won 220–215 in 2024 and sit at 218–214 with two vacancies, so Democrats need a net gain of three or four. Every forecaster puts them well past it. The question is size: Cook's range is 230–240, Sabato's final map has 239 seats at least leaning Democratic, Decision Desk HQ and Silver Bulletin land on 240–241, and the Economist's median is a gain of 37. The desk's 236 is deliberately a little lower.

Where the forecasters land

Central Democratic seat estimates, final publications. 218 is a majority; 235 is what Democrats won in 2018; 257 is the 2008 high-water mark.

The redistricting war, state by state

After the Supreme Court narrowed Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act in Louisiana v. Callais (29 Apr 2026), Republican legislatures in Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana and Alabama redrew on top of the 2025 Texas, North Carolina and Ohio maps. California answered with Prop 50 (64–36) and a Utah court imposed a Democratic Salt Lake seat. Missouri's map was suspended pending tonight's Prop A referendum; Virginia's Democratic map (+4 D) was struck down in May; Indiana, Kansas and South Carolina Republicans failed to pass theirs; the Supreme Court blocked New York's redraw. On paper the surviving maps are worth about R+9 (Ballotpedia counts R+15 gross against D+6). In this environment Decision Desk HQ expects the realized effect to be roughly zero or slightly Democratic, because the new Texas and Florida seats were drawn for a Trump +5 world, not a D+9 one.

Axios's accounting of the hurdle: Democrats needed to outrun Harris's 2024 district margins by 3.1 points before redistricting and 4.9 after; Harris-won districts fell from 205 to 200. A D+9 generic ballot clears that with room to spare, which is why every model is at 84–96%.

The dynamics underneath

  • Crossover seats. Under current lines Democrats hold 11 Trump-won districts and Republicans hold only three Harris-won ones (PA-1, NY-17, CA-48; NE-2 is open). The maps turned several Democratic seats into Trump districts (NC-1, AL-2, TX-28, TX-34, FL-9, FL-22).
  • Retirements. 60 members plus two delegates are leaving, 26 Democrats and 36 Republicans, the second most since 1992; 29 are running for other office.
  • History of toss-ups. In midterms, about 40% of Cook's toss-ups go to the president's party (FiftyPlusOne). Applied to 18 toss-ups that is roughly 11 Democratic wins; applied to Inside's 82 competitive seats it is the heart of the 20–30 range.
  • What counts as a good night. Democrats: 218–225 is control but an under-performance; 230–240 is Cook's range; more than 235 beats 2018; 240+ is the largest majority since 2009. Republicans: holding 218 is a 5% upset; keeping Democrats under about 228 means the Texas and Florida floor did its job and 2028 is in reach.
  • Nathan Gonzales, Inside Elections: "2026 feels similar to 2006," when Democrats gained 30 seats. Dave Wasserman, Cook: 230–240, and "anything less than 10 would be shocking."
