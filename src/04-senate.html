Senate, seat by seat

53 R · 45 D · 2 I today. 33 Class 2 seats plus the Ohio (Vance) and Florida (Rubio) specials. Final ratings: Cook and Sabato 2 Nov, Inside Elections 29 Oct

Republicans defend 22 seats and Democrats 13. The map is the one Democrats have wanted since 2020: eight Republican seats with no incumbent running (Alabama, Iowa, Kentucky, Montana, North Carolina, Wyoming by retirement; Texas and Louisiana because John Cornyn and Bill Cassidy lost their primaries; Oklahoma's appointee was barred from the ballot) and a ninth, South Carolina, held by an appointee after Lindsey Graham died in July.

The races that decide it

North Carolina · Cooper (D) v Whatley (R) · open, Tillis retiredCook Likely D · Sabato Likely D · IE Lean D · Desk 95% D

The likeliest flip on the board. RCP has Cooper +10.7; WRAL/WSOC 54–40; AtlasIntel 56–42. Cooper had raised $47.2M to Whatley's $13.4M by 30 September and the Senate Leadership Fund paused its North Carolina spending on 2 October. Republicans run Whatley, the former RNC chair, as Trump's ally; Democrats run Cooper on two terms as governor. Signal: polls close 06:00 IST and the count is fast. A Cooper margin under 5 means the late Democratic surge is overstated; 10 or more and the reach seats are live.

Ohio special · Sherrod Brown (D) v Jon Husted (R, appointed)Cook Toss-up · Sabato Lean D · IE Tilt D · Desk 70% D

RCP Brown +2.6, Silver Bulletin +3.0. Times/Siena moved from R+3 in June to Brown 49–44 in its final poll; Suffolk 47–44; Rasmussen the outlier at Husted 49–47. The most expensive race in the country at $435M, with the Senate Leadership Fund alone past $80M. Brown lost in 2024 by running behind only Trump's coattails; this is a midterm against an appointee. Husted's close: Brown "is driving up the costs". Brown's: the cost of living. Signal: early and absentee votes report first and lean Democratic, so expect Brown ahead early and Husted closing. A call around 08:30–10:30 IST if the margin is about 3. A Brown win makes 51 likely; a Husted hold means Democrats probably need Kansas or Iowa.

Maine · Susan Collins (R) v Troy Jackson (D)Cook Toss-up · Sabato Lean D · IE Toss-up · Desk 60% D

Jackson, a logger and former state Senate president, became the nominee by convention in July after Graham Platner won the primary with 72% and withdrew over sexual-assault allegations he denies (Janet Mills had suspended her campaign in April). RCP has Jackson +1.7, Silver Bulletin +4.0; Times/Siena's final poll 51–45 Jackson after showing Collins +3 in September; UNH 54–45 and AtlasIntel 53–44 for Jackson, Emerson 51–49 Collins, Fox and Trafalgar tied. Outside groups have spent $137M+, over half attacking Jackson. Joe Manchin endorsed Collins. Why the desk is below the market: Collins ran roughly eight points ahead of her 2020 polls. Signal: polls close 06:30 IST; the count is slow. Jackson by more than 5 means the Collins discount is gone and the night is strong for Democrats. A Collins win makes the Democratic path run through Texas, Kansas and Alaska.

Texas · James Talarico (D) v Ken Paxton (R) · open, Cornyn lost the runoffCook Toss-up · Sabato Lean D · IE Tilt D · Desk 55% D

Paxton beat Cornyn "by a landslide" in the 26 May runoff after Trump endorsed him; Talarico beat Jasmine Crockett in the Democratic primary. RCP Talarico +1.8, Silver Bulletin +3.5; Times/Siena 50–43, CNN 49–43, AtlasIntel 52–46, Quantus Paxton +1, Rasmussen tied. Talarico has raised $158M+ to Paxton's $39M, but super PACs including Musk money carry Paxton. Early vote passed 7.7M by 30 October (40.7% of registered voters); total turnout may top 10M. Republicans close on "Talarico's Texas or our Texas" and tax cuts; Democrats on "the most corrupt politician in Texas". Why the desk is below the market: Texas polls overstated Democrats by about three points in both 2020 and 2024, and the state voted Trump +14 two years ago. Signal: polls close 06:30 IST (El Paso 07:30). The early-vote dump comes first and leans Democratic; rural Election Day votes come later. A call around 10:30–12:30 IST if the margin is 3 or more. Watch the Rio Grande Valley: a Talarico win would be the first Democratic Senate win in Texas since 1988 and would mean the Hispanic swing back is real.

Alaska · Mary Peltola (D) v Dan S. Sullivan (R) · top-four ranked choiceCook Toss-up · Sabato Lean D · IE Toss-up · Desk 60% D

RCP Peltola +1.2, Silver Bulletin +2.6; Times/Siena 48–46 Peltola in its final poll after 50–43 in September. Two complications: the ballot also carries "Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.", a retired teacher with no party polling above 4%, whom Republicans call a sham candidate; and on 1 November Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed a Justice Department suit, filed on Trump's instruction, that sought to delay certification if Sullivan loses. DOJ appealed within the hour. Signal: first results after 11:15 IST on Wednesday; no call on the night. Ranked-choice tabulation is scheduled for 18 November, certification about 25 November. Alaska could decide control late, and in court.

Kansas · Adam Hamilton (D) v Roger Marshall (R)Cook Toss-up · Sabato Lean D · IE Toss-up · Desk 45% D

Kansas has not elected a Democratic senator since 1932. Hamilton, founding pastor of the country's largest United Methodist church and a former Trump voter, leads RCP by 2.6 and Silver Bulletin by 3.3; Times/Siena 49–44, Emerson 50–47, CNN +4, Washington Post +9 to +10 (the Post itself warned its sample over-represented Democrats), InsiderAdvantage Marshall +1. $85M spent, the most in Kansas history. Marshall calls Hamilton's Epstein-files attack a stunt; Hamilton runs on prices, Marshall's vote to table the Epstein amendment and an ethics complaint. Kalshi's Hamilton price went from 39% to 63% in a week. Why the desk tilts R: Trump +16 state, a Libertarian taking 3–4%, and the Post poll's own caveat. Signal: polls close 06:30 IST (west 07:30). Johnson County, the Kansas City suburbs, decides it. A Hamilton win is a wave-level result; a Marshall hold is the Republican firewall story.

Iowa · Josh Turek (D) v Ashley Hinson (R) · open, Ernst retiredCook Toss-up · Sabato Lean R · IE Toss-up · Desk 42% D

The closest race on the map: RCP Hinson +0.2, Silver Bulletin Turek +0.7, so the two averages point opposite ways. Times/Siena 48–47 Turek; Rasmussen, co/efficient and InsiderAdvantage Hinson +1 to +2; Cook's own poll Turek +2; Suffolk Turek +4; Marist Turek +3. Iowa voted Trump +13 and Republicans hold a 180,000 registration edge, but independents break for Turek, a Paralympic gold medallist with the Farmers Union endorsement, by double digits. Trump rallied in Dubuque on 1 November. Signal: polls close 07:30 IST; Iowa votes mostly on the day, so the count favours Republicans late. A call may not come until Wednesday evening IST. Watch the eastern river counties (Dubuque, Scott, Linn). A Turek win is the clearest wave marker and points to 53+ Democratic seats.

Michigan · Abdul El-Sayed (D) v Mike Rogers (R) · open, Peters retiredCook Lean D (from Toss-up, 2 Nov) · Sabato Lean D · IE Tilt D · Desk 78% D

The one realistic Republican pickup. Rogers, a former FBI agent and House Intelligence chair, lost here by 0.3 in 2024. Times/Siena has El-Sayed 53–42, CNN 49–42, Silver Bulletin +4.7. On Sunday 1 November Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the primary to El-Sayed, endorsed Rogers and appeared with him in Detroit; El-Sayed raised $1.2M off it and his Kalshi price dipped then set a new high near 78%. Cook moved the race to Lean D the next day citing new public and private polling. Signal: polls close 06:30 IST; Detroit, Wayne and Oakland absentee processing is slow and a call may slip to Wednesday if the margin is under 4. A Rogers win cuts the Democratic net and could cost them the chamber.

South Carolina · Darline Graham (R, appointed) v Annie Andrews (D)Cook Lean R · Sabato Lean R · IE Tilt R · Desk 25% D

Lindsey Graham won his primary with 57% and died in office on 11 July; Governor McMaster appointed his sister, Darline Graham Nordone, who then beat Rep. Ralph Norman 52–48 in the August special runoff. Norman has refused to campaign for her. RCP Graham +2.7, Silver Bulletin +1.3; Times/Siena tied 48–48; East Carolina 51–47 Graham; Trafalgar +3. She runs nearly double digits behind the Republican governor nominee. Andrews, a paediatrician, runs on petrol and grocery prices and on a seat "handed" by Washington. Signal: polls close 05:30 IST, the first real contest of the night. A quick Republican call means a normal night; "too close to call" past about 07:30 IST is the first wave alarm.

Nebraska · Pete Ricketts (R) v Dan Osborn (independent) · no Democrat on the ballotCook Lean R · Sabato Lean R · IE Tilt R · Desk: Osborn 30%

The Democratic nominee withdrew in July. Osborn led YouGov 48–47 and SurveyUSA 46–42 in September, but Times/Siena's final poll has Ricketts 49–43 and co/efficient 48–43; the trend is toward Ricketts. A Republican super PAC committed $15M and Senate Republicans $8M more. Osborn's caucus intentions are not confirmed, so an Osborn win is a seat lost by Republicans, not a clean Democratic gain. Signal: polls close 07:30 IST; Osborn needs big margins in Omaha (Douglas) and Lincoln (Lancaster). A call is likely around 10:30 IST.

Georgia, New Hampshire, Minnesota · the Democratic holdsGA Likely D · NH Lean/Likely D · MN Lean/Likely D

Georgia: Jon Ossoff leads Rep. Mike Collins by 8.2 in RCP (AJC 48–40, InsiderAdvantage 53–46). The risk is a 1 December runoff if he finishes under 50%; he polls at or near it. New Hampshire: Chris Pappas leads John E. Sununu 48–41 (UMass Lowell/YouGov) and UNH's final poll says he "continues to lead"; fast town-by-town count, call likely by 09:30 IST. Minnesota: Peggy Flanagan leads Michele Tafoya 54–40 in Times/Siena but only 45–42 in an earlier KSTP/SurveyUSA poll; Republicans see "a tide within the state" after fraud scandals (Kornacki). A Republican over-performance in any of these would be the Sun Belt or Upper Midwest warning the GOP case needs.
