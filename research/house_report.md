## 2. MID-DECADE REDISTRICTING: STATE BY STATE

**Background.** On **29 April 2026**, in *Louisiana v. Callais*, the US Supreme Court ruled Louisiana's map an unconstitutional racial gerrymander and partially curtailed VRA Section 2's majority-minority requirement (Wikipedia, citing NPR/ABC). That opened the way for the Louisiana and Alabama redraws and coincided with Florida's map. Wikipedia notes that many primaries had already started, which blunted the ruling's effect on 2026.

### Maps IN FORCE for 2026

**Texas (Republican legislature, +5 R target).** Called into special session after Trump asked; Gov. Abbott signed the map **29 Aug 2025**. A three-judge federal panel blocked it on 18 Nov 2025, and SCOTUS stayed that ruling 6–3 and allowed the map on **4 Dec 2025**. **In force.** Displaced Democratic incumbents: Veasey, V. Gonzalez, Doggett, Julie Johnson and Al Green (Wikipedia TX page). On-paper targets per DDHQ (14 Sep): **TX-9** (new Houston-area R seat, open; Gutierrez-D vs Mealer-R), **TX-35** (new San Antonio seat; Casar moved to TX-37), **TX-28** (Cuellar) and **TX-34** (Gonzalez), both made redder. Two other seats matter here. **TX-15** (R, De La Cruz) was reinforced to Trump +18. The fifth target was in DFW (the Veasey/Johnson seats); exact district number not confirmed. DDHQ's average expected *realized* gain was **R +1.6 to just under +2**, about three short of the goal. Inside Elections (31 Oct): "at least three of the seats Republicans were counting on… are not looking easy for them."

**California (Prop 50, Democratic response, +5 D target).** Voters approved it **4 Nov 2025**, 64.4–35.6 (Wikipedia; Ballotpedia rounds to 65–35). A three-judge panel upheld it in January 2026, and SCOTUS denied the GOP application **4 Feb 2026**. **In force.** Targeted R seats:
- **CA-1:** LaMalfa died; James Gallagher (R) won the special. Rated Solid D (flip) by Cook, Sabato and Inside.
- **CA-3:** Kiley's old seat, now Ami Bera (D). Solid D (flip).
- **CA-6:** Kiley (NPP) vs Richard Pan (D). Cook Solid D. Sabato and Inside Likely D.
- **CA-22:** Valadao. Cook Toss-up, Sabato Lean D.
- **CA-48:** Issa's seat, now open. Cook Lean D, Sabato Likely D.
- Calvert's CA-41 was also reconfigured; DDHQ notes Calvert/Kim shifts in CA-40/41.

DDHQ (14 Sep) expected about **D +4**. DDHQ's election-eve guide says Democrats are "favored to flip all five GOP-held House seats that they targeted."

**Florida (Republican legislature; DeSantis signed 4 May 2026; +4 R target).** **In force**; Ballotpedia gives no court block. Targets:
- **FL-9** (Soto): redrawn from Harris-won to about Trump +18 (WLRN).
- **FL-14** (Castor).
- **FL-22:** Frankel moved to FL-23; now Trump +10+, previously Harris +5 (WLRN).
- **FL-25** (Moskowitz).

Ballotpedia lists +4 R; Wikipedia's table lists +3 R. DDHQ (14 Sep) expected the net effect to be about **zero**, because Democrats gain chances in other R seats (FL-7, FL-13, FL-27).

**Ohio (bipartisan Redistricting Commission, unanimous, 31 Oct 2025; +2 R on paper).** **In force**, locked until 2032. It made **OH-1** (Landsman) and **OH-9** (Kaptur) more competitive, and OH-13 (Sykes) slightly bluer. DDHQ expected "almost no change." Cook moved OH-9 to Lean D on 30 Oct.

**North Carolina (Republican legislature, 22 Oct 2025; law without the governor's signature; +1 R).** A federal panel allowed it on 26 Nov 2025. **In force.** Target: **NC-1** (Don Davis), now Trump +12. DDHQ expected net **D +1** in the state.

**Tennessee (Republican legislature; Gov. Lee signed 7 May 2026; +1 R).** Judge Campbell declined to block it on 26 May. **In force.** Target: **TN-9** (Cohen's Memphis seat, open), aiming for a 9–0 delegation. Inside Elections moved TN-9 from Solid R to Likely R on 31 Oct.

**Louisiana (Republican legislature after Callais; SB 121 signed 29 May 2026; +1 R).** **In force** for 2026; Wikipedia says challenges are pending. Target: **LA-6** (Cleo Fields's seat, reduced Black population). DDHQ: GOP "all but guaranteed" the new 6th.

**Alabama (+1 R).** On 2 June 2026, SCOTUS (6–3) lifted the *Milligan* injunction, so the legislature's 2023 map is in use (Ballotpedia; Wikipedia). Target: **AL-2** (Shomari Figures), now Trump +14 per DDHQ. Final ratings: Cook Lean R, Inside Tilt R, Sabato **Leans D** (moved from Toss-up on 2 Nov).

**Utah (court-ordered map; +1 D).** Judge Gibson adopted plaintiffs' Map 1 on 10 Nov 2025. The Utah Supreme Court rejected the appeal on 20 Feb 2026, and a repeal measure failed to qualify on 26 Mar 2026. **In force.** It creates a safely Democratic **UT-1** around Salt Lake City (DDHQ).

### Maps passed but NOT in force, or failed

**Missouri (Republican legislature; Gov. Kehoe signed 28 Sep 2025; +1 R target against Cleaver's MO-5).** Referendum signatures were filed 9 Dec 2025. On 3 Sep 2026 the Missouri Supreme Court put the referendum (**Prop A**) on the 3 Nov ballot. On 25 Sep, SCOTUS stayed contrary rulings, so the **2022 map is used for November** (Ballotpedia, updated 28 Oct). **Not in force.** Voters decide the map's future tonight.

**Virginia (Democratic legislature plus referendum; target 6D-5R → 10D-1R, about +4 D).** Spanberger signed the map 21 Feb 2026, and voters approved the amendment **21 Apr 2026** (51.6–48.4). The Virginia Supreme Court struck it down **8 May**, and SCOTUS denied a stay **15 May**. **Not in effect**; the old 6–5 map stands.

**Maryland (Democratic target: Andy Harris's MD-1).** The House of Delegates passed a map 99–37 on 2 Feb 2026. Senate President Ferguson refused a Senate vote. An Aug 3–5 special session handled only a constitutional amendment for the Nov 3 ballot; Ferguson's stated aim is to protect the current 7–1 map (Bethesda Magazine, 3 Aug). **No new map.** MD-1 is rated Likely R by Sabato.

**Other states:**
- **Indiana (Republican, targeting IN-1/IN-7):** The House passed a map 5 Dec 2025; the Senate rejected it 11 Dec 2025, with 21 Republicans and all Democrats voting no. **Failed.**
- **Kansas (Republican):** Stalled; about "20 votes short" as of 6 Jan 2026. **Failed.**
- **South Carolina (Republican, targeting Clyburn):** Passed the House in May 2026 and failed in the Senate. **Failed.**
- **New York:** A court struck Malliotakis's NY-11; **SCOTUS blocked the redraw on 2 Mar 2026**, so the old lines stand.
- **Colorado:** The state Supreme Court ruled on 29 Jun 2026 against mid-decade redistricting. Wikipedia notes a GOP ballot measure aimed at 2028. No change for 2026.
- **Wisconsin, Arkansas:** Challenges failed; maps stand.
- **Washington, New Jersey, Illinois, New Hampshire, Maine:** Discussed but not pursued for 2026.
- **Georgia, Mississippi, Nebraska, Kentucky:** No enacted 2026 redraw found.

## 4. BIG-PICTURE DYNAMICS

**Crossover seats.** Wikipedia (Oct): under current lines, **11 Trump-won districts are held by Democrats**, and 3 Harris-won districts are held by Republicans. It also counts 11 redrawn districts Trump would have won and 5 redrawn districts Harris would have won. Kondik's Feb 2026 count, before the FL/TN/LA/AL maps, was 8 R-held Harris seats and 16 D-held Trump seats. Named R-held Harris seats include **PA-1, NY-17, CA-48**, and NE-2 (open).

**Retirements and open seats.** Wikipedia: **60 representatives plus 2 delegates, 26 D and 36 R** (including the delegates). Of these, 29 are seeking other office (9 D, 20 R). It is the second-most in a cycle after 1992's 65. AP: 16% of incumbents will not return.

**Generic ballot (final reads):**
- Silver Bulletin: **D 52.8–43.4 (D+9.4)**, ~1–2 Nov via The Conversation.
- Wikipedia average as of 25 Oct: **D+8.7** (DDHQ D+8.0, FiftyPlusOne D+9.2, NBC D+9.0, RCP D+8.3, Silver D+9.0).

**Break-even after redistricting.** Axios (7 Jun 2026): Democrats needed to outrun Harris's 2024 district margins by **3.1 pts before redistricting, 4.9 pts after**. Harris-won districts fell from **205 to 200**. Before Missouri and Virginia were reversed, the gerrymanders added roughly 1.8 points to the Democratic hurdle. A D+8 to D+9 generic ballot clears that by a wide margin, which is why every model has Democrats at 84–96%.

**What counts as a good night:**
- **Democrats:**
  - 218–225: control, but an underperformance against the ratings.
  - 230–240: the Cook range.
  - **More than 235:** beats 2018 (Sabato's benchmark).
  - 240+: largest majority since 2009, per Cook ("if they start to win Lean or even Likely R… more like a tsunami").
  - 257: Obama's 2008 high-water mark, which Cook calls unlikely.
- **Republicans:**
  - Holding 218: an upset; the markets price it at about 5–6%.
  - Keeping Democrats under about 228: a containment win that preserves a 2028 path. This is near DDHQ's September baseline of 229, and the redistricting floor (TX, FL, TN, LA, AL) would have done its job.

**The Republican case (as its proponents make it):**
- Mid-decade maps in Texas, Florida, Ohio, North Carolina, Tennessee, Louisiana and Alabama bank several seats.
- Polls may understate GOP support, and "GOP-leaning undecided voters will turn out and reluctantly support GOP candidates" (Gonzales, Inside Elections, 31 Oct).
- The Lawler campaign argues that Democratic spending against strong incumbents is wasted and that races are "shifting in our favor" (2 Nov).
- Sabato concedes it "wouldn't take much—some understatement of Republicans in polls combined with strong GOP Election Day turnout—for Republicans to save some of these races, and even their majority." That line is about the Senate, but it reflects the same dynamic.

**The Democratic case (as its proponents make it):**
- Trump's approval is weak, and there is an unpopular Iran war and cost-of-living pressure (CBS).
- Rating moves were one-way: Sabato made 66 moves toward Democrats versus 2 toward Republicans since 1 Oct, and Inside made 63 toward Democrats versus 1 in its final week.
- Democrats lead the generic ballot by about 9 and outraised Republicans in Q3 (Kondik).
- The GOP has pulled spending from key contests (The Hill), and CLF cancelled $6M in Miami TV (WLRN).
- Strong top-of-ticket Democrats (Cooper in NC, Brown in OH, Talarico in TX) drag red seats into play.
- Midterm toss-ups historically break against the White House: about 40% go to the president's party (FiftyPlusOne's analysis of Cook data).
- Gonzales: "2026 feels similar to 2006," when Democrats gained 30 seats.

  - SC-1, KY-6.

Bellwether: Collin Co., TX.

- **9 pm:** 15 states, including **AZ** (AZ-1, 2, 6), **IA** (IA-1, 2, 3), **MN-1**, **NY** (NY-17, 3, 4, 19, 21), **WI** (WI-3, WI-1), **CO** (CO-3, 4, 5, 8), NE (NE-2), and the rest of MI/TX/KS. Bellwether: Dubuque Co., IA.

- **12–1 am:** AK-AL, HI.

**Slow counts (likely days):** California (mail-heavy; CA-22, CA-13, CA-48), Washington (WA-3), Arizona, Nevada, Maine (ME-2; "just about the slowest-counting state outside of those that mostly use vote-by-mail"), and Alaska ("could take a couple of weeks"). Ranked-choice in ME-2 and AK-AL can add tabulation days. New York's close races (NY-17) may wait on absentee cures.
