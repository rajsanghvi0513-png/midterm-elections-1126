## 0. Corrections to the brief's assumptions (read first)

Several things changed after mid-2026. The desk should know these before air:

- **Maine:** Collins's Democratic opponent is **Troy Jackson**, a former state Senate president. It is not Janet Mills or Graham Platner. Mills suspended her campaign on 30 Apr 2026. Platner won the 9 Jun primary with 72%, then withdrew on 8 Jul after sexual-assault allegations, which he denies. A state convention then nominated Jackson by 566 votes to 5 (Wikipedia).
- **South Carolina:** **Lindsey Graham won his 9 Jun primary (57%), then died in office on 11 Jul 2026.** On 13 Jul, Gov. McMaster appointed his sister, **Darline Graham Nordone**, styled "Darline Graham" on the campaign. The name looks odd but is correct (Daily Signal, WTTW, Bloomberg Government, all 13 Jul). She beat Rep. Ralph Norman 52–48 in the 25 Aug special GOP runoff. Her opponent is Democrat Annie Andrews, and the race has become competitive.
- **Texas:** **Cornyn lost.** Ken Paxton beat him "by a landslide" in the 26 May runoff, after Trump and Wesley Hunt endorsed Paxton on 19 May. The Democratic nominee is **James Talarico**, who beat Jasmine Crockett in the primary.
- **Louisiana:** **Cassidy lost renomination**, finishing a distant third. Rep. **Julia Letlow** (Trump-endorsed) beat John Fleming in the GOP runoff. The Democrat is Jamie Davis.
- **Oklahoma:** Markwayne Mullin became Homeland Security Secretary (confirmed 23 Mar 2026). Appointee Alan Armstrong was barred from running. The GOP nominee is Rep. Kevin Hern; the Democrat is N'Kiyla "Jasmine" Thomas. The seat is safe R.
- **Michigan:** The Democratic nominee is **Abdul El-Sayed**, who narrowly beat Haley Stevens. On 1 Nov, **Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers**.
- **Kansas:** Pastor **Adam Hamilton (D)** has made this a genuine toss-up against Sen. Roger Marshall.
- **Nebraska:** Democratic nominee Cindy Burbank withdrew on 17 Jul. It is a two-way race between Ricketts and independent **Dan Osborn**.
- **Iowa:** The brief was right that Ernst retired. The race is Ashley Hinson (R) vs Josh Turek (D).
- **Confirmed as assumed:** NC is Cooper vs Whatley. Ohio special is Husted vs Sherrod Brown. Florida special is Ashley Moody vs **Angie Nixon** (Nixon beat Alex Vindman on 18 Aug). Alaska is Sullivan vs Peltola. Georgia is Ossoff vs Rep. Mike Collins. NH is Chris Pappas vs **John E. Sununu**. Minnesota is Peggy Flanagan vs Michele Tafoya. Kentucky is Andy Barr vs Charles Booker.

## 1. Senate math

- **Composition entering the election:** 53 R, 45 D, 2 I. Both independents (King of Maine, Sanders of Vermont) caucus with the Democrats, so the caucuses stand at 53 R and 47 D. Two appointments this year kept the seats Republican: Armstrong in Oklahoma and Darline Graham in South Carolina. *Sources: Wikipedia; Around Prague guide, checked 30 Oct.*
- **Seats up: 35.** These are all 33 Class 2 seats plus two Class 3 specials:
  - **Ohio:** Vance's former seat, held by appointee Jon Husted.
  - **Florida:** Rubio's former seat, held by appointee Ashley Moody.
  - Both specials are for terms ending in January 2029. Your assumption was correct. Oklahoma and South Carolina are regular Class 2 elections that happen to have appointed incumbents.
- **Who defends what:** Republicans defend 22 seats (20 Class 2 plus FL and OH). Democrats defend 13. *Wikipedia; WBAL/KCRA live updates, 3 Nov.* An older 270toWin page header said 23 R, but every current source says 22.
- **What Democrats need: a net gain of 4, for 51 seats.** A net gain of 3 produces a 50–50 Senate, and VP JD Vance's tiebreak would keep Republicans in control.
- **Open seats:** Democrats have four (IL, MI, MN, NH). Republicans have eight non-running situations:
  - Retirements: AL, IA, KY, MT, NC, WY.
  - Appointee not on the ballot: OK.
  - Incumbents who lost primaries: TX (Cornyn) and LA (Cassidy).
  - South Carolina's appointee is running.

## 3. Deep dives

### Texas: Paxton (R) vs Talarico (D). Open seat; Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean D / IE Tilt D

- **Polls.** RCP had Talarico +2.1 (8–27 Oct) and +1.8 (48.8–47.0) on 30 Oct. Silver Bulletin has Talarico +3.5.
  - NYT/Siena: tied in June, D+6 in September, D+7 in the final poll (Talarico 50–43, 27 Oct–1 Nov, n=1,215).
  - CNN/SSRS: Talarico 49–43 (20–27 Oct).
  - AtlasIntel: Talarico 52–46.
  - Quantus Insights (30 Oct): Paxton about 49–48.
  - Rasmussen (24 Oct): tied 45–45.
- **Money.** Talarico has raised more than $158M; Paxton about $39M. Paxton is backed by super PAC money, including "tens of millions" from Elon Musk. *The Texan, 3 Nov.*
- **Early vote.** 7.7M+ ballots cast early through 30 Oct, about 40.7% of registered voters. Total turnout could exceed a record 10M. *The Texan.*
- **Central dynamic.** Paxton carries impeachment-era corruption baggage, and the GOP is divided after his runoff win. Sabato told CNN on 2 Nov that Cornyn and his supporters "are not enthusiastic" about Paxton. Talarico's favourability has been dented by GOP attacks on his past progressive statements.
- **Republican closing case.** "Talarico's Texas or our Texas": Talarico is too extreme, and Paxton promises more tax cuts (Spectrum News, 30 Oct).
- **Democratic closing case.** "Defeat the most corrupt politician in Texas," plus repeal of tax cuts for the wealthy and universal child care.
- **What it signals.** A Talarico win would be the first Democratic Senate win in Texas since 1988 and would point to a wave that reached Hispanic South Texas. A comfortable Paxton win would suggest polls overstated Democrats again, as in 2020 and 2024, which would be bad news for Democrats in KS, IA and SC.

### Maine: Collins (R) vs Troy Jackson (D). Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean D / IE Toss-up

- **Polls.** RCP had Jackson +1.7 (5–26 Oct). Silver Bulletin has Jackson +4.0 (50.3–46.3).
  - NYT/Siena trend: D+2 in June (vs Platner), R+3 in September, D+6 in the final poll (51–45, 27 Oct–1 Nov).
  - Late October: Marist Jackson +2; CNN Jackson +4; Fox tied 49–49; Trafalgar tied.
  - Listed by RCP on 2 Nov: UNH Jackson 54–45; AtlasIntel Jackson 53–44; Emerson/Poll Vault **Collins 51–49**.
- **Money.** Outside groups have spent more than $137M, over half of it attacking Jackson (OpenSecrets via Boston Globe, 2 Nov). Campaign totals: not found.
- **Central dynamic.** Collins outran polls badly in 2020. The question is whether her brand survives a Democratic environment and a Harris +6.9 state. Jackson, a logger and former Senate president, replaced Platner in July.
- **Cross-party endorsement.** Former Democratic Sen. Joe Manchin, now an independent, endorsed Collins in mid-September (Political Wire, 14 Sep; Semafor, 15 Sep, says he planned to campaign for her).
- **Republican closing case.** Seniority and delivery for Maine: "I don't work for any president. I work for you." Collins also touts Canada-tariff exemptions she won for Maine (Newsweek).
- **Democratic closing case.** A referendum on Trump and on Senate control, with a class-based anti-billionaire message.
- **What it signals.** Jackson winning by more than 5 points would mean the "Collins polling discount" is gone and would point to a strong Democratic night. A Collins win would mean ticket-splitting still exists and would make the Democratic path dependent on TX, KS and AK.
- **Count.** RCV matters only if no one tops 50%; no notable third candidate was found. Maine counts slowly but not as slowly as Alaska.

### Ohio special: Husted (R) vs Sherrod Brown (D). Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean D / IE Tilt D

- **Polls.** RCP had Brown +2.5 (23 Sep–22 Oct) and +2.6 (30 Oct). Silver Bulletin has Brown +3.0.
  - NYT/Siena trend: R+3 in June, D+3 in September, Brown 49–44 in the final poll (26 Oct–1 Nov).
  - Suffolk: Brown 47–44 (21–26 Oct).
  - InsiderAdvantage: Brown +3.
  - Rasmussen: **Husted 49–47** (12–14 Oct).
  - co/efficient: Husted +1 (5–6 Oct).
- **Money (VoteLog, 24 Oct).** Total spending is $435.2M.
  - Brown raised $68.4M; Husted $19.9M (FEC through 14 Oct).
  - Senate Leadership Fund alone had spent $80.4M.
  - WinSenate had spent $64.1M.
- **Central dynamic.** Brown lost in 2024 by running behind nothing but Trump's coattails. This is a midterm with a sitting appointee.
- **Republican closing case.** Husted says Brown "is driving up the costs" and ties him to national Democrats.
- **Democratic closing case.** Brown leads on the cost of living.
- **What it signals.** Ohio closes at 7:30 ET and counts fairly quickly, so it is an early tell. A Brown win would mean the Rust Belt has swung back and makes 51 likely. A Husted hold would mean Democrats probably need Kansas or Iowa.

### North Carolina: Whatley (R) vs Cooper (D). Open seat (Tillis retired); Cook and Sabato Likely D (flip), IE Lean D (flip)

- **Polls.** RCP had Cooper +10.7. WRAL/WSOC: Cooper 54–40 (mid-October). AtlasIntel: Cooper 56–42. The final Elon poll gave Cooper a double-digit lead (20 Oct). Carolina Journal (October) had his lead "narrowing to 10."
- **Money.** Through 30 Sep, Cooper had raised $47.2M with $12.5M on hand; Whatley had raised $13.4M with $916K on hand (WRAL). Senate Leadership Fund paused NC ad spending in early October (CNN, 2 Oct).
- **Central dynamic.** This is the likeliest Democratic flip. The GOP has effectively triaged it.
- **Republican closing case.** Whatley, a former RNC chair, has run as Trump's ally.
- **Democratic closing case.** Cooper runs on his two terms as governor.
- **What it signals.** Polls close at 7:30 and the count is fast. If Cooper wins by under 5 points, the late Democratic surge is overstated. If he wins by 10 or more, expect the reach seats to be live.

### Michigan: Rogers (R) vs El-Sayed (D). Open seat (Peters retired); Cook Lean D (2 Nov), Sabato Lean D, IE Tilt D

- **Polls.** NYT/Siena: El-Sayed 53–42 (23 Oct–1 Nov, fielded before the Stevens episode); D+5 in September. CNN/SSRS: 49–42. Silver Bulletin: El-Sayed +4.7. Co/efficient (R) also polled the race; numbers not found.
- **Stevens episode.** On Sunday 1 Nov, Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the primary to El-Sayed, endorsed Rogers and appeared with him in Detroit. Reports say this followed a Democratic feud involving a church event; details were not fully verified.
  - Kalshi's price dipped, then hit a new high of about 78%.
  - El-Sayed raised $1.2M off the endorsement (The Hill).
- **Republican case.** Michigan is the GOP's only realistic pickup. Rogers is a former FBI agent and House Intelligence chair, running against a progressive the GOP calls too far left. He lost by only 0.3 points in 2024. Republicans hope Stevens's move unlocks suburban Democrats.
- **Democratic case.** The environment plus El-Sayed's improved standing with Black voters, which Cook flagged.
- **What it signals.** A Rogers win would cut the Democratic net and could cost them control. An El-Sayed double-digit win would confirm the NYT/Siena picture.

### Iowa: Hinson (R) vs Turek (D). Open seat (Ernst retired); Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean R / IE Toss-up

- **Polls.** This is the closest race. RCP has **Hinson +0.2** (1–31 Oct); Silver Bulletin has **Turek +0.7**, so the two averages point in opposite directions.
  - NYT/Siena trend: R+2, R+1, then Turek 48–47 (26 Oct–1 Nov).
  - Rasmussen: Hinson 47–46 (27–31 Oct).
  - co/efficient: Hinson 48–46 (28 Oct).
  - InsiderAdvantage: Hinson +1.
  - Cook Political Report's own poll: Turek +2.
  - Suffolk: Turek 48–44 (1–4 Oct).
  - Marist: Turek +3.
- **Money.** Not found.
- **Central dynamic.** Iowa voted Trump +13 in 2024, and the GOP has about a 180,000-voter registration edge. Independents break for Turek by double digits in most surveys (Latin Times). Trump rallied in Dubuque on 1 Nov.
- **Republican closing case.** Hinson frames it as "common sense versus crazy" and has shifted late from "Trump's top ally" to "bipartisan dealmaker."
- **Democratic closing case.** Turek, a Paralympic gold medallist, has the Iowa Farmers Union endorsement and is running on farm-economy stress.
- **What it signals.** A Turek win would be the clearest wave marker; Democrats would likely land at 53 or more. A Hinson win is consistent with a normal Democratic year.

### Kansas: Marshall (R) vs Adam Hamilton (D). Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean D / IE Toss-up

- **Polls.** RCP has Hamilton +2.6 (22 Sep–28 Oct). Silver Bulletin has Hamilton +3.3.
  - NYT/Siena: tied in September, Hamilton 49–44–4 in the final poll (27 Oct–1 Nov).
  - Washington Post: Hamilton +9/+10 (22–26 Oct).
  - CNN: Hamilton +4.
  - Emerson: Hamilton 50–47 (26–28 Oct).
  - InsiderAdvantage: **Marshall +1** (13–14 Oct).
  - A YouGov poll released on 1 Nov had a 1-point gap; direction not reported.
- **Money (VoteLog, through 14 Oct).** Hamilton raised $19.2M; Marshall $5.35M. Total spending is $85.2M, the most expensive race in Kansas history (Kansas Reflector).
- **Central dynamic.** Kansas has not elected a Democratic senator since 1932. Hamilton is the founding pastor of the nation's largest United Methodist church and a former Trump voter. Libertarian David Graham's roughly 3–4% may matter.
- **Republican closing case.** Trump rallied in Salina on 1 Nov and praised Marshall's farm-bill work. Marshall calls Hamilton's Epstein-files attack a "political stunt."
- **Democratic closing case.** Hamilton runs on the cost of living, Marshall's vote to table the Epstein-files amendment, and an ethics complaint over Marshall's use of taxpayer money.
- **Markets.** Kalshi's Hamilton price jumped from 39% (26 Oct) to 63% (2 Nov).
- **What it signals.** A Hamilton win is a wave-level result. A Marshall hold is the GOP's firewall in the narrative that "red states held."

### Alaska: Sullivan (R) vs Peltola (D). Top-four RCV; Cook Toss-up / Sabato Lean D / IE Toss-up

- **Polls.** RCP has Peltola +1.2 (polls 1–16 Oct). Silver Bulletin has Peltola +2.6.
  - NYT/Siena trend: R+2 in June, Peltola 50–43 in September, Peltola 48–46 in the final poll (23–30 Oct).
- **Complications.**
  - The ballot carries "Dan S. Sullivan" (the incumbent, R) and "Daniel J. Sullivan Jr." (no party). The challenger, a retired Petersburg teacher, polls above 4%. Republicans call his candidacy a "sham."
  - On 1 Nov, Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed a DOJ suit, filed 22 Oct and ordered by Trump, that sought to delay or block certification if Sullivan loses. DOJ filed an appeal within about an hour.
  - FBI agents reportedly visited donors to the challenger. *Alaska Public Media, 1 Nov.*
- **Money.** Not found.
- **What it signals.** No call is likely on the night. The RCV tabulation is scheduled for **18 Nov** and certification is targeted for about 25 Nov (Around Prague). Alaska could decide control late and in court.

### South Carolina: Darline Graham (R) vs Annie Andrews (D). Cook and Sabato Lean R, IE Tilt R

- **Polls.** RCP has Graham +2.7 (28 Sep–26 Oct). Silver Bulletin has Graham +1.3.
  - NYT/Siena: tied 48–48 (19–26 Oct).
  - East Carolina University: Graham 51–47.
  - The Citadel: Graham 43–39.
  - Trafalgar: Graham +3.
- **Dynamic.** Graham is badly underperforming the GOP governor nominee, Alan Wilson, by nearly double digits (Post and Courier). Rep. Ralph Norman, whom she beat in the runoff, has refused to campaign for her.
- **Republican closing case.** At Trump's Rock Hill rally on 29 Oct, Graham called Andrews "bat[expletive] crazy." Trump urged a vote in tribute to Lindsey Graham. Her campaign says its internals match the public polls.
- **Democratic closing case.** Andrews, a paediatrician, runs on gas and grocery prices and on "not handed the seat by Washington."
- **Money.** Not found; Graham attacks Andrews's "Hollywood" donors.
- **What it signals.** Polls close at 7:00 ET. An early Andrews lead or a near-tie would be the night's first wave alarm for the GOP.

### Nebraska: Ricketts (R) vs Dan Osborn (I). Cook and Sabato Lean R, IE Tilt R

- **Polls.**
  - NYT/Siena: Ricketts 49–43 (23–29 Oct).
  - co/efficient: Ricketts 48–43 (28–30 Sep).
  - YouGov: Osborn 48–47 (24 Sep–3 Oct).
  - SurveyUSA: Osborn 46–42 (8–13 Sep).
  - The trend has moved toward Ricketts.
- **Money.** A GOP super PAC committed $15M to defend Ricketts. Senate Republicans added a further $8M (Yahoo; Bloomberg Law). Cornhusker Majority spent about $7M against Osborn (Legis1).
- **Caucus question.** No Democrat is on the ballot. Osborn's intentions on caucusing were not verified in this research. Treat any Osborn win as a seat lost by the GOP, not a clean Democratic gain.

### Georgia: Ossoff (D) vs Mike Collins (R). Likely D across raters

- **Polls.** RCP has Ossoff +8.2. AJC: 48–40 (7–15 Oct). InsiderAdvantage: 53–46. Research Co.: 52–42.
- **Risk.** Georgia goes to a runoff on **1 Dec** if no one reaches 50%. Ossoff is polling at or near 50%.
- **What it signals.** An Ossoff win above 50% on the night would remove the runoff scenario. A Collins over-performance would be a warning across the Sun Belt.

### Shorter notes

- **New Hampshire** (Lean D / Likely D / Tilt D). UMass Lowell/YouGov: Pappas 48–41 (released 29 Oct). The final UNH poll said Pappas "continues to lead." Sununu's family brand is the GOP's case. Polls close at 8 ET and the count is fast.
- **Minnesota** (Lean D / Likely D / Lean D). Siena: Flanagan 54–40. KSTP/SurveyUSA: Flanagan 45–42 earlier in October.
- **Florida special** (Likely R everywhere; Fox Solid R). Siena: Moody 50–44. Polls close at 7 ET and the count is fast. A narrow Moody margin under 4 points would point to a big night.
- **Montana.** Four-way race: Alme (R), Bodnar (I), Bankhead (D), Austin (L). MSU-Billings: Alme +4 over Bodnar. Cook and Sabato Solid/Safe R; DDHQ Likely R (77%). The split anti-Alme vote favours the Republican.
- **Mississippi: Hyde-Smith (R) vs Scott Colom (D), with Ty Pinkins (I).** Likely R almost everywhere; IE moved it from Solid R on 29 Oct. Trump rallied for Hyde-Smith in Corinth on 26 Oct. The race has drawn late attention because of one poll and one hoax:
  - **The real poll.** The only public survey is from a Democratic firm: Change Research for the 365 Project (21–22 Oct) had Hyde-Smith 43, Colom 40, Pinkins 6 (IBTimes).
  - **The fake poll. Do not quote it.** Wedgewood Polls released Hyde-Smith 52–48 (12–14 Oct). The firm then admitted it fabricated the data as a stunt, and Colom's campaign had promoted it as "tied" (Magnolia Tribune, 20 Oct). Any average that still contains Wedgewood polling, such as Poliwave's Hyde-Smith +3.3, is contaminated.
  - **History.** In 2020, polls showed Mike Espy within 1 point of Hyde-Smith, and he lost by 10.
  - Polymarket has D at 14% (coordinator, 3 Nov).
- **Louisiana: Letlow (R) vs Jamie Davis (D).** IE Likely R; Sabato Lean R. No public polling found. A wave-only watch; Polymarket D 12.5%.
